Master's Thesis

Meshing Methodology for Numerical Simulations of Turbulent Flows at Low Computational Cost

Overview: I earned a master's degree at the National University of Colombia, where I focused on computational meshing strategies for a compressible supersonic jet from a nozzle. The objective was to test resolution effects and mesh refinement regions to improve the numerical accuracy of computational simulations based on experimental data and LES simulations carried out by other researchers. By developing optimized low-cost meshes under the UDNS framework, I found that first- and second-order turbulent flow characteristics could be predicted without explicit turbulence models.

Parameters such as mesh size, axial and radial refinement, and the computational domain size played a critical role in accurately predicting key turbulence statistics, including energy dissipation, turbulence intensity, and Reynolds stresses. The academic contribution of this work lies in demonstrating how mesh topology, resolution strategy, and domain design influence the predictive capability of CFD simulations. These findings provide insight into mesh sensitivity and highlight effective refinement strategies, rather than uniform refinement.

I used OpenFOAM to construct structured computational meshes based on meshing parameters. I used ParaView as my primary post-processing tool to analyse turbulence fields and velocity gradient isocontours. Moreover, I developed Python scripts to export numerical data, wrote Fortran programs to perform statistical analyses, and used Gnuplot to plot velocity curves and turbulent intensities. These experiences were crucial in enhancing my computer programming skills in a Linux environment, from setting up simulations and automating statistical calculations to analysing large datasets and presenting results.

Computational Work

Transient Simulations

This section presents examples of transient simulations related to my research and previous coursework.

Supersonic jet

Computational simulation using the finite volume method in OpenFOAM of the instantaneous axial velocity and temperature fields of a supersonic round jet generated by a nozzle.

Transient of the Diffusion-convection equation

Numerical solution of the diffusion-convection equation with the finite element method. An implicit method for time integration and a Peclet number less than 1 were employed. In advance, the numerical solution of a potential flow in the computational domain was computed.

Elastodynamics with FEM 3

Numerical solution using the finite element method to solve the elasticity equation with hexahedral elements of second order. Damped oscillation of a tuning fork after it has been moved from its equilibrium position.
